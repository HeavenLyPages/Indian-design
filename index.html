Agra · 27.17°N 78.04°E

Five thousand years,
in one scroll.

You cannot understand a monument without the layers underneath it. This is the short version — the one our guides actually tell.

c. 2600 BCE · Bronze Age

The Indus Valley

Harappa and Mohenjo-daro were running on grid-planned streets, covered drains and standardised bricks while most of the world was still building with mud. The script they left behind is still undeciphered.

c. 600 – 321 BCE · Iron Age

The Mahajanapadas

Sixteen competing kingdoms across the Gangetic plain. Magadha wins, the Upanishads are written, and the Buddha and Mahavira both teach within the same century.

321 BCE · Maurya Empire

Chandragupta & Ashoka

The first empire to hold nearly the entire subcontinent. Ashoka, after Kalinga, renounces conquest and carves his edicts into rock pillars — the four lions of Sarnath are his, and now the republic's.

320 – 550 CE · Gupta Era

The Golden Age

Kalidasa writes, Aryabhata calculates the earth's rotation, zero finds its first full expression, and the first free-standing stone temples are raised at Sanchi and Deogarh.

1206 – 1526 CE · Delhi Sultanate

Stone, in conversation

Five dynasties rule from Delhi. Qutb Minar rises, Persian arches meet Indic trabeation, and a genuinely new architectural language is invented — one we still walk through today.

1526 – 1857 CE · Mughal Empire

The Mughals

Babur, Akbar, Shah Jahan. Fatehpur Sikri, the Taj Mahal, miniature painting, and an administrative machine that outlived the dynasty by a century. The scale of ambition is still visible in every red sandstone wall.

1857 – 1947 CE · The Long Century

Revolt, then freedom

The uprising of 1857 reshapes colonial rule. Ninety years later, a non-violent mass movement — the largest in recorded history — ends it. Partition draws two borders in five weeks.

1947 – Today · The Republic

The largest democracy

Twenty-two scheduled languages, a constitution written by hand, and a space programme that put a probe around Mars on the first attempt. India is not a country you finish; it is one you keep returning to.

Mumbai · 18.92°N 72.83°E · The Story

The people
behind it.

Six of us, based between Mumbai and Jaipur, plus a network of guides we have known for years. Call any of us — there is no call centre.

Safar started in 2014 as two friends driving a rented Bolero through Rajasthan with four strangers who had answered a posting on a travel forum. It went badly enough to be funny, and well enough that we did it again the next year.

Eleven years later we run 48 routes and still cap every departure at nine travellers. We do not own hotels. We do not take commissions from shops. We pay our guides a monthly salary, which in this industry is rarer than it should be.

If something goes wrong on a trip — and occasionally it does — you will be talking to one of the six people on this page, not a support ticket.
